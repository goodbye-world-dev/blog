Last week I took part in the Agentic SDLC hackathon. For a few hours we tried to automate as much of our daily programming work as possible. I'll be honest — those few hours with pizza and "programming" rewired my entire AI worldview. For the first time in years I saw clearly that this isn't about making things better, easier, or more comfortable for us programmers. It's about making us unnecessary.

I thought I had the theory of programming with AI fairly well figured out. Sure, practice always takes a back seat — but it turned out my thinking about AI in programming is *sooo* 2025.

Some companies show off AI at conferences and on posters while in practice blocking their own employees from visiting anything under a .AI domain. Others reply to emails with ChatGPT and feel thoroughly modern, while their developers are still dreaming of access to Claude Code. Then there are companies like iteratec, where we've had access to plenty of tools for a long time — but our mindset is still rooted in good old-fashioned programming.

The revelation from the hackathon is obvious, but brutal. This isn't about how AI should *support* programming — it's about how AI should *replace* programmers. The fact is, our role lately has been reduced to typing prompts at Claude, and that's pretty straightforward to automate. Of entire teams, one or two people will remain as the mythical human in the loop. The day the last living programmer makes the last commit is still far off — but that's the inevitable direction.

All that's left is to orchestrate the whole thing and turn off the lights.

You might say that working on the orchestration of such a solution is sawing off the branch you're sitting on. A very comfortable branch at that — one you've been sitting on for many years. The truth is, the world won't miss us programmers all that much. Yerba producers will shed a tear, but they'll quickly find other wealthy fans of hot herbal brews. Because business will keep building software — just instead of paying us, it'll pay for tokens (and tokens don't need fruit Fridays or gym memberships). The age is coming for people with ideas who will quickly ship more, cheaper software. Maybe the world will be happier for it.

Obviously a major banking system won't write itself tomorrow — but the question is no longer *if*, only *when*. Let's not be like an Uber driver waiting for autonomous cars so he can sleep at the wheel. Let's acknowledge that these are our final chords — let's play them well and in tune, and think about what comes next.

My programmer branch has been, and still is, very comfortable — I really don't want to lose it. But if it has to be cut, I'd rather help the process along. I'll put the agents to work on my tasks, watch projects build themselves, make that last commit someday. I'll step off the stage just a little bruised, execute a double Auerbach flip and land a clean telemark in a different reality. I'll stop here for today — I have agents to drill and, uh... flips to train.

Thanks for making it to the end.
